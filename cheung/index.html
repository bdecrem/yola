Research

His areas of research are syntax, semantics, corpus linguistics and natural language processing (NLP). He works on grammatical phenomena across Cantonese, Mandarin and English. Specific topics include right dislocation structures, wh-placeholders, the negative wh-construction, sentence-final particles, telicity marking, egophoricity and statistical analysis of grammatical variations.

He has pursued a corpus-based approach to grammar, automatic linguistic annotation and syntactic parsing using machine learning. In recent years he joined interdisciplinary projects applying linguistic analysis and AI to Alzheimer’s disease and autism.

Syntax

Sentence structure across Cantonese, Mandarin and English, including right dislocation and the syntax–phonology interface.

Semantics

Meaning in language, including the negative wh-construction, wh-placeholders, telicity marking and egophoricity.

Corpus linguistics

Research corpus approach to grammar, automatic linguistic annotation and tagging, and Chinese morphology recognition.

Natural language processing

Statistical analysis of grammatical variation and syntactic parsing using machine learning, with AI applied to Alzheimer’s and autism research.
